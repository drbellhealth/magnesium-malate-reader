Magnesium Malate Ingredients

A line-by-line look at what's inside Designs for Health Magnesium Malate Chelate, including active components and excipients.

The full ingredient list is on the bottle and the brand's site. The short version is below — there is not much to it, and that is by design.

Active Ingredients

What is actually in it:

  • Di-magnesium malate — magnesium bound to malic acid; the chelated form is chosen for better absorption and an easier ride on your gut than cheap magnesium oxide
  • Elemental magnesium per serving — the real number is on your current label, so check the bottle, since the serving size has changed over the years while the malate form stayed
  • Malic acid — a natural part of the body's energy cycle; this is the reason malate gets chosen when the issue is fatigue rather than sleep
  • Nothing else added — no extra vitamins, minerals, or herbs; it is a single-ingredient magnesium, not a blend
  • Practitioner-grade chelate — sourced from established chelate makers and made to cGMP standards

Other Ingredients (Excipients)

The non-active ingredients are short and clean: a plant-based (hypromellose) capsule shell plus a little microcrystalline cellulose, vegetable stearate (the plant version of magnesium stearate), and silicon dioxide. No added sugar, no artificial colors or flavors. If you are avoiding a specific filler, your current label is the place to check, since they update it across reformulations.

Allergens and Sensitivities

Magnesium Malate is labeled gluten-free and non-GMO, and the capsule is plant-based, so it works for vegetarians and generally for vegans. It is usually free of dairy and soy, but if you have a specific allergy, confirm against the current label, which carries the official allergen statement. There are no stimulant ingredients in it.

Sourcing and Quality Notes

Designs for Health is a US practitioner brand based in Connecticut that has been in the clinical world for decades and sells mostly through licensed providers and authorized distributors. They make to cGMP standards and use chelated magnesium from established suppliers rather than the commodity oxide kind. Buying through the practitioner channel or the brand's own store is the most reliable way to avoid the diverted or expired stock that sometimes shows up from random marketplace sellers. How Ingredients Compare to Similar Products

Where malate sits compared with the other magnesium forms: oxide is the cheap drugstore one, barely absorbed and basically a laxative; citrate is well-absorbed but the most reliably laxative, good for constipation and bad if loose stools are your problem; glycinate is calming and gentle, the one people take at night for sleep — Designs for Health makes a separate glycinate for that; L-threonate is the pricey brain-marketed one. Malate, the one here, is gentle on your gut like glycinate but is the daytime pick because of the malic-acid energy link, which is why it gets recommended for fatigue and muscle complaints rather than sleep. None is best for everyone — it depends on what you want and how your gut handles it.
